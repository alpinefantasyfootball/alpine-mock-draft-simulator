Privacy Policy

What Juke does and doesn't do with anything of yours

Juke has an account system now, and it's the one thing in this document that changed since the last version: email plus a one-time sign-in link, never a password, and nowhere in the app that asks for a card number. What follows is a complete, specific account of what the app actually does, not a template filled in with the usual disclaimers.

Written to describe what the code actually does rather than to read like a generic policy — every claim below was checked against the source and the live configuration before being written down. It hasn't been reviewed by a lawyer. Last updated 29 August 2026, for the account system this version documents for the first time.

01A mock draft on your own

Drafting solo, the whole app runs in your browser and nothing about your draft is sent to a server at all — there is no server in that path to send it to. The board, the projections, the scoring and the grade are all computed on your own machine from files the page already downloaded. This is still true whether or not you're signed in — an account adds a second, synced copy of what follows below; it never changes how a draft itself is computed.

Your progress — the seat you picked, every pick made, your league settings, your queue and watchlist, and any draft you've finished — is written to your browser's own local storage (localStorage), on your device only. Signed out, Juke has no way to read it back, because there is nowhere for it to be read back from except the browser it was written on. Clearing your browser's site data deletes it permanently, the same as clearing it for any other site. Section 02 covers what changes, and what doesn't, once you sign in.

02Signing in

An account is optional, and every draft feature works exactly the same without one — unlimited mock drafts, custom scoring, drafting with friends, your locker of saved drafts. The only thing an account adds is that your locker follows you between devices instead of living in one browser.

There's no password anywhere in Juke, on purpose: you enter an email address, we send a one-time link, and clicking it signs you in. That link works once and stops working after fifteen minutes, whether or not you clicked it. Signing in creates an account holding exactly three things:

  • the email address you signed in with;
  • when you created the account;
  • your locker — your saved in-progress draft and your completed mocks, the same data section 01 describes, copied to our server so a second device can see it too.

Nothing else. No password to store because none is ever set, no name, no payment details, no address.

The first time you sign in on a browser that already has mocks saved locally, those mocks are copied into your new account — you're told how many, on screen, when it happens. That copy runs once per account, ever; signing in again on the same browser, or on a different one, never re-copies or duplicates it. Signing out clears the sign-in from that browser but leaves its local mocks exactly where they were — signing back in reconnects to the same account and the same locker, it doesn't create a second copy.

You can delete your account at any time from the account menu, with no need to email anyone. Deleting removes your email address, your account record, and your entire server-side locker immediately and permanently. It does not touch mocks already saved in the browser you're using — only what was copied to our server goes away, for the same reason signing out doesn't erase your local locker either.

Signing in and staying signed in is handled by a long-lived credential stored in your browser's local storage, the same way the room id in section 03 is — not a cookie, and not sent to any server besides ours. Signing out (or deleting your account) invalidates it immediately; it also expires on its own after ninety days of not being used.

03Drafting with your league

Sending an invite link creates a shared room, and a room is the one case where drafting does involve a server — everyone in it has to agree on what happened, which your browser alone can't arbitrate. While that room is open, it holds:

  • who's sitting in which seat, and the display name each person typed in;
  • every pick that's been made;
  • the chat messages sent in that room.

A room doesn't know or care whether you're signed in. Your browser generates a random id the first time you join one, stored the same way as everything in section 01, and that id — not your account, not your email address — is what the room uses to tell you apart from the person in the next seat.

A room isn't kept forever. Once every participant has been disconnected for six hours, the room and everything in it — seats, picks, chat, display names — is deleted automatically.

04What your browser talks to directly

A few things load straight from someone else's server, the same way an embedded video or a web font does on most sites — that server sees the request come from your browser directly, including your IP address, the same as it would for any site using it:

Player photosSleeper's own image server, linked directly rather than copied onto ours.
Live scoresESPN's public scoreboard, only fetched while there are games actually being played.
FontsGoogle Fonts (Barlow Condensed, Inter, Archivo).

None of these get anything from Juke beyond the ordinary fact that your browser asked for a file — no draft data, no room data, nothing you've typed.

05What goes through Juke's own server

Three things are proxied or handled through Juke's server instead of being called directly, specifically so a provider's own access key never has to sit in a page your browser can read:

  • Player news headlines, from Tank01 — fetched when you open a player's sheet, cached briefly (fifteen minutes) so the same headline isn't fetched fresh from the provider every time.
  • GIF search in room chat, from GIPHY — only if you actually search for one.
  • The sign-in email itself, sent through Resend when you request a sign-in link. Your browser never talks to Resend directly — it asks our server for a link, our server asks Resend to deliver it, and the only thing Resend is told is the one address you typed and the email's content.

All three carry Juke's own server as the visible caller, not your browser, and none is told anything about you beyond what's needed to answer that one request (a player id, a search term, or the address to send one email to).

06Hosting, and Cloudflare's own analytics

The site is hosted on Cloudflare, which sees the same standard request information — your IP address, browser and the page requested — that any hosting provider sees for any site it serves. Separately, Cloudflare injects its own Web Analytics beacon into every page on this site to measure real-world load times and errors. That beacon is Cloudflare's, added at their network edge rather than by anything in Juke's own code; it doesn't use cookies and isn't the kind of beacon that follows you across other, unrelated sites.

07What we collect, and what we never do

What section 02 already says in full, restated here so it sits beside the "never" list it used to stand alone against: if you create an account, we hold your email address, when you created it, and your locker. Nothing else is ever asked for, account or no account:

  • No password, ever — there is nowhere in the app to set or type one.
  • No payment information. Juke is free and nothing in it is for sale.
  • No advertising trackers, and nothing here is sold or shared with anyone for marketing.
  • No location data beyond the coarse, IP-level location any web request already carries.
  • No name, no phone number, no address of any kind — an email is the one identifying thing an account ever holds.

08Cookies

Juke's own code sets none, signed in or out. Everything in sections 01 and 02 — your local draft data and, if you sign in, the credential that keeps you signed in — is held in local storage, which is not a cookie and is never sent to a server automatically the way a cookie is. It only leaves your browser when the app deliberately sends it, as in a room or when it checks you're still signed in.

09Children

Juke isn't directed at children and isn't built to identify anyone's age — signing in only ever asks for an email address. It's a fantasy football tool, built with an adult fantasy-football audience in mind.

10Changes to this page

If what the app actually does changes, this page changes with it — the same rule the rest of the app follows: nothing here should describe a version of Juke that no longer exists. The date at the top moves when it does.
