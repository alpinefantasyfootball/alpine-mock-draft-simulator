OPTION A vs OPTION C — SAME SURFACES, BOTH PALETTES

This is not a change of colour. It is a change of saturation.

Your teal sits at hue 186°. The shark's aqua sits at hue 180°. Six degrees apart — the same colour family. What actually separates them is that yours is at 100% saturation and the shark's is at 64%.

WHAT THAT MEANS FOR THIS DECISION

Option A is not a compromise on the shark's colour — it is the shark rendered in the saturated version of its own hue. And Option C is not "new brand colours"; it is your existing brand, desaturated and lightened, with the purple removed. Every difference you see below comes from those two moves.

What I would do

AMY PICK

You lose nothing. The shark's hue is preserved — it just arrives at full saturation, which is where a fantasy product wants its accent. Purple survives, so the gradient, the hover wash and the two-colour app tile all keep working.

The position palette is untouched and keeps sitting on a neutral ground, which is what your own notes say it was derived against.

Scope is one file's internals plus an icon re-export. You could ship it this week.

C

C is genuinely more sophisticated. It is calmer, more premium, and closer to a design-led sports brand than an app. If the goal were a magazine, I would pick it.

But it costs you a second accent hue, and your interface uses that hue for real work. And your own DESIGN-DIRECTION.md already names atmosphere as the gap — desaturating the palette moves away from that, not toward it.

If you do want C, the honest sequence is to take it as its own project after the logo lands, not bundled with a mark swap.

THE THIRD ANSWER, IF YOU WANT MOST OF C

Keep #00E5FF and the purple as they are, and use the shark's #002F3F only as a surface tint on the marketing pages — the hero, the Rooms, the splash. The product stays electric where the work happens, and the brand reads tidal where you are selling. That gets you the premium feel of C without touching a single contrast derivation in the draft room.
